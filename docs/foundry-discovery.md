## Idea brief

**Working name:** Memoir

**Who I want to help:**
Grown children, roughly 35 to 60, whose parents or grandparents are getting
older and who want those people's stories kept before they're gone. The
person telling the stories is usually 70 or older and will not type, install
an app, or sit down to write.

**The problem they experience:**
Everyone means to record their parents' stories and almost nobody does. The
tools ask the wrong person to do the work: a 78-year-old is handed a writing
prompt or a phone app, gets stuck, and stops. The stories that get lost are
the ordinary ones, told in that person's own voice, and you only notice
they're gone afterwards.

**How they solve it today:**
- They don't. "We should really record Grandpa" and then it never happens.
- Phone voice memos that never get transcribed, organized or shared.
- StoryWorth (about $99 a year): weekly email prompts and a printed book at
  the end. It works for parents who will write; many won't.
- Voice-based services such as Remento and Storii, which show the demand is
  real.

**My proposed approach:**
Voice first, no typing, nothing for the parent to set up.
1. The grown child sets it up and picks the prompts ("How did you meet
   Mom?", "What was your first job?").
2. The parent answers by talking: into one big button on a link, and later
   on an ordinary phone call.
3. Memoir transcribes it, keeps their words rather than rewriting them,
   and organizes the stories into chapters on a timeline.
4. The family gets both the recording and the text, and can download the
   whole thing or print it as a book.

Built today (memoir.creativekonsoles.com): voice capture, transcription,
AI-organized entries, the timeline. Not built yet: family accounts, the
link the parent answers through, phone-call answering, sharing, the book.

**The riskiest assumption:**
That an older parent will actually record stories when the only effort is
talking — week after week, not just once. Second: that the grown child,
not the parent, is the one who pays.

**The smallest experiment to test it:**
Five families. The grown child sets up three prompts; the parent answers
by voice over two weeks. Measure: how many parents finish all three, how
long the answers run, and whether the child shares them with anyone else
in the family. Done by hand where the product isn't built yet.

**What evidence would change my mind:**
- Fewer than 3 of 5 parents finish their prompts, even with help.
- Families like the recordings but nobody would pay, or only for a one-off
  gift and never for an ongoing subscription.
- Parents only open up on a live call with a person, which would make this
  a service rather than software.

**My next action and date:**
Recruit the five families by **Friday, October 9, 2026** and start the
two-week test the following Monday.

## Positioning and offer brief

**For this specific audience:**
Adult children with aging parents or grandparents, especially the family
member who already keeps the photos and organizes the holidays.

**Who needs this outcome:**
The family, to keep a parent's life story in that parent's own voice. The
parent, to be asked and listened to.

**We offer:**
The easiest way to record a parent's stories: they just talk, and Memoir
turns it into a family memoir with the audio and the words kept together.

**Unlike the current alternative:**
StoryWorth and similar services ask the parent to write. Memoir asks them to
talk, which is how those stories have always been told. Unlike voice memos,
nothing ends up lost on a phone: every story is transcribed, organized and
shared.

**Our evidence or reason to believe:**
- Paying competitors (StoryWorth, Remento, Storii) show families already
  pay for this.
- A working prototype is live today: speak, and get organized, readable
  entries back.
- Founder story: I built Memoir because I live with a cognitive condition
  that affects memory. It started as a way to keep my own story.
- No evidence yet from real families. The five-family test is how we get it.

**What is included:**
Story prompts the family chooses; talk-to-record with no typing; transcripts
in the parent's own words; chapters on a timeline; the original audio kept;
sharing with family; a full download.

**What is excluded:**
- No ghostwriting: we organize their words, we don't invent new ones.
- No medical or memory-care claims. This is a family keepsake, not therapy
  or dementia care.
- No public feed: stories are private to the family.
- No printed book in the first version (it comes later, as an add-on).

**How to try it:**
The first story is free: one prompt, one recording, the transcript sent to
the family. Then a subscription.

**How we will test willingness to pay:**
- Ask each of the five test families directly at the end of the two weeks:
  would you pay $99 for a year (StoryWorth's price) or $49 for one parent's
  stories?
- Put a pre-order button on the landing page at $99 a year and count real
  clicks.
- Test it as a gift: a holiday gift link (Mother's Day, Father's Day,
  Christmas) and see whether it converts better than buying for yourself.
